## AI and theoretical computer science

We discussed both the opportunities and the risks of using AI in theoretical computer science (TCS). AI can help us learn and make progress in research, but we must retain responsibility for our own thinking, understanding, and the work we share.

### Opportunities for learning and research

Many areas of TCS have a steep learning curve. AI can help students work through unfamiliar material and mathematical tools more quickly, making it easier to learn what they need and begin applying it to research. Used thoughtfully, it can help researchers achieve better results faster.

### Independent thinking and research credit

We need to be conscious of the risk of letting AI replace our own thinking and understanding. Guiding an AI through many conversations toward a research goal can create a false sense of ownership of the result. That process alone does not justify taking credit for research whose technical details we do not understand and whose correctness we cannot check. We should be able to explain the reasoning ourselves and evaluate whether it is sound.

### Our no-AI-slop policy

Our team does not allow sharing AI-generated research material that we have not understood and checked. Fear of being scooped does not justify rushing an unchecked, AI-generated paper into public view.

It is fine to keep AI-generated ideas and drafts privately while taking the time to digest them. Before sharing or publishing the resulting work, we must understand the techniques, check correctness, and present and write it based on our own understanding. Producing a polished-looking document is not a substitute for doing that work.
