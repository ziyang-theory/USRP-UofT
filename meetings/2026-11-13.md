# Project proposals and research directions

**Date:** Friday, November 13, 2026

Ziyang discussed each member's project proposal in turn. We explored several possible research directions and discussed how to develop an initial idea into a concrete project.

## Possible project directions

1. **Study the computational complexity of a real-world problem.** Choose a problem you care about, define it precisely, and investigate its complexity. Examples include NP-hardness results for generalized [Super Mario Bros. (FUN 2014)](https://erikdemaine.org/papers/Nintendo_FUN2014/) and [Tetris (COCOON 2003)](https://erikdemaine.org/papers/Tetris_COCOON2003/). These results concern families of instances with growing levels or boards; the cited Tetris result assumes the full piece sequence is given in advance.
2. **Formally verify a classic TCS result.** Develop a machine-checked proof of a theorem, such as Savitch's theorem, using a proof assistant. Check existing formalizations to identify what your project could add.
3. **Explore AI-agent collaboration using cryptography.** How can AI agents collaborate while protecting their owners' sensitive information? Investigate tools such as secure multiparty computation (MPC) and zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs). Specify the collaborative task, the sensitive information, and what each agent should be allowed to learn. One option is to [extend Ziyang’s SecCol paper](../mentor/projects/seccol/README.md).
4. **Develop an idea from an earlier discussion.** For example, revisit the questions about bounds on the size of Condorcet winning sets and algorithms for finding small winning sets. See the [October 23 discussion](2026-10-23.md#potential-research-directions-condorcet-sets).

## Preparation for next week

Before **Friday, November 20, 2026**, each member should:

1. **Spend several hours refining a project proposal.** Narrow the topic to a specific question and consider how you could approach it.
2. **Search the literature.** Identify the closest existing work and what it achieves. Aim for a project that improves on the state of the art or investigates something that has not been done before. Use the literature to support the proposed novelty.
3. **Prepare a one-page project proposal, with a maximum of two pages.** State the problem, summarize the closest prior work, explain the intended new contribution, and outline your approach and first steps. Bring the proposal to the next meeting.
