### Intro to the Course

TCS stands for Theoretical Computer Science. Furthermore the course focuses in Algorithms and Computational Complexity.

These subjects are topics published in the top conferences in TCS, that being STOC and FOCS.

Topics in STOC/FOCS are what the professor is going to refer to when lecturing. So the main idea of these lectures are to get the student know more about TCS research and also be able to understand advanced topics when encountering them in research seminars, talks, etc... The purpose is to help the student develop background knowledge in TCS.

The content is biased by the professor's own research interest which generally include probability and complexity. More breadth than depth, which makes it better for starting students wanting to get deep into TCS. Mathematical Maturity is important.

From my point of view the syllabus shows that the course is focused to give the student a broad scope of knowledge so that the student can focus in what to focus whilst not risking not having any knowledge in other fields of TCS.

### How to do TCS

This is a part which mostly focuses in the philosophy behind TCS, and not necessarily, as the title suggests, "How to do TCS", since as the professor mentioned, it is a bit pretenious.

"The following is what the professor thinks regarding TCS and Math, these are not generalized views:"

1. TCS has primarily mathematics as part of its foundation, thus making it very closely to it.
2. TCS is way better than mathematics because of the following reasons

   -Because you can study whatever you want. Because mathematics graduate student in the end of their academic trajectory are a world expert in some tiny corner of mathematics and you keep doing and studying for the rest of your life, because that individual got this far.

   -TCS is better, because computation is at the heart of every science.

   -Every mathematical topic has some computational aspect to it. The professor mentions how TCS was used to disprove mathematical theorems using complexity.

Some disadvantages to TCS are what anyone would expect, difficulty including struggling in research and many other things.

Some anecdotes and sayings from people like Julia Robinson, Terence Tao, and the professor himself.

### Advice

An advice is that when struggling with problems, take some time off from that problem and read, write, watch, learn or look into some other parts of mathematics and TCS.

TCS blogs, announcements( this helps to be kept up to date with the recent events that unfold in the TCS world): https://theory.report and https://cstheory.stackexchange.com.

Attend talks, research seminars, etc...

Another form of staying au courant is to watch videos of talks, talks in various institutions like those in UofT, MIT, CMU, UCB, etc...

### Papers

You can find them in various conferences, such as FOCS/STOC/SODA/CCC. This is the base place.

Other places include: arXiv and ECCC. 

A good thing is to analyze papers, meaning who cites the paper you are reading and also what papers does the paper you are reading cite. Maybe the best way to do this is through using Google Scholar and follow the above procedure.

### Lifetime things an individual interested in TCS research should start doing

#### Manage references

Such processes include:

-Save a local copy of a paper you read
-Use consistent naming convention
-Maintain a lifetime .bib file for all papers you ever reference.
-Use a reference management software. These include: JabRef, BibDesk, etc...

#### LaTeX

If you are interested in TCS, research, mathematics, then you MUST become fluent in LaTeX.

A really good place to ask questions about LaTeX is the following website: https://tex.stackexchange.com/

##### LaTeX Editor
