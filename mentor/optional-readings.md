# Optional reading materials

The [shared roadmap](roadmap.md) suggests a weekly plan for exploring theoretical computer science (TCS). Adjust the pace and topics to your time and interests as you find areas you want to study.

If you prefer a different topic, tell Ziyang what you propose to read from this list or another source. Share notes from your own thinking in your [mentee folder](../mentees/).

- [*Algorithmic Game Theory*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/algorithmic-game-theory/0092C07CA8B724E1B1BE2238DDD66B38), edited by Noam Nisan, Tim Roughgarden, Éva Tardos, and Vijay V. Vazirani. Look under the publisher's Resources tab for online access. If you need a downloadable edition, check [U of T Libraries](https://library.utoronto.ca/use/tool/librarysearch).
- [Tim Roughgarden's Algorithmic Game Theory lectures](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLEGCF-WLh2RJBqmxvZ0_ie-mleCFhi2N4), from Stanford's CS364A course.
- [*Computational Complexity: A Modern Approach*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/computational-complexity/3453CAFDEB0B4820B186FE69A64E1086), by Sanjeev Arora and Boaz Barak. You can use the [free author draft](https://theory.cs.princeton.edu/complexity/), which is marked preliminary. A published edition from U of T Libraries is preferable when available. [Arora notes](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/spring16/cos522/) that later printings corrected many typographical errors in earlier printings.
- [Ryan O'Donnell's Graduate Complexity Theory at CMU lectures](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLm3J0oaFux3b8Gg1DdaJOzYNsaXYLAOKH), from CMU's 15-855 course.
- [*A Graduate Course in Applied Cryptography*](https://toc.cryptobook.us/), by Dan Boneh and Victor Shoup.
- [*A Pragmatic Introduction to Secure Multi-Party Computation*](https://securecomputation.org/docs/pragmaticmpc.pdf), by David Evans, Vladimir Kolesnikov, and Mike Rosulek. Chapters 1 and 2 are an alternative to Lectures 5 and 6 for the [October 23 meeting](roadmap.md#week-3).
- [*A Proof of Security of Yao's Protocol for Two-Party Computation*](https://eprint.iacr.org/2004/175), by Yehuda Lindell and Benny Pinkas.
- [*How To Simulate It – A Tutorial on the Simulation Proof Technique*](https://eprint.iacr.org/2016/046.pdf), by Yehuda Lindell.
